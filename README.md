## 🧭 What I do

I lead the software team at [EmbeddedSystems.do](https://embeddedsystems.do/), building environmental and IoT
monitoring products: Python and React web apps on AWS, data pipelines, dashboards and cloud tooling.
Before that came five years of quantum-information research at the University of Gdańsk, which is where I learned to
turn abstract questions into results anyone can reproduce.

## 🌊 Featured: BENTOS

An open-source water-quality platform for the Gulf of Gdańsk, developed by EmbeddedSystems.do with the
[Institute of Oceanology, PAS](https://www.iopan.pl/). It brings together in-situ sensors, satellite observations and
predictive modelling. I build the software side: data pipelines, APIs and dashboards.

<summary><b>🧱 About this repository</b></summary>

<br />

The source of the portfolio site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step, no dependencies.

- Hero with a live [Clifford attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_attractor) on `<canvas>` (reacts to the mouse, pauses off-screen, static with `prefers-reduced-motion`). The drifting parameters now and then brush a periodic window where particles used to merge for good and thin out to a few dots; a few are re-seeded every frame and the drift fast-forwards through a collapse
- Bento-grid projects, a BENTOS feature in the project's navy/lime branding, publications, timeline and contact
- Holographic project cards: textured cardstock, engraved foil motifs with latent images that appear under the light, and spectral reflections that follow the pointer. Touch and reduced motion keep them flat
- A collection of thirteen attractor trading cards (Clifford, Peter de Jong, Simon, Ikeda, Julia, Thomas, Field–Golubitsky, Gumowski–Mira, Bedhead, McMullen, a harmonic IFS and more), each with a classic holo foil, as on printed trading cards: in the artwork, the attractor is cut out and the space around it catches the light as holo bars, an etched fingerprint texture or sun pillars; on the frame, reverse holo and gold cards carry a texture drawn from that card's own mathematics (woven update fields, escape-time contours, flow lines…), while regular holo cards keep a matte frame. An iridescent edge ring and colour sweeping over the type finish it. All foils are generated by `assets/deck/foil/generate.py`. The cards also show the render, the equations as iteration rules and the parameters. Cards tilt on springs and flip with a button, the mouse wheel, `F`, the arrow keys or a drag. `js/deck.js` and `css/deck.css` load only on first use; renders come from the chaosv2 notebooks and `ifs_complex.py`, exported to `assets/deck/`
- A faint synthwave horizon sits behind the About text (pure CSS), the “Currently learning” card is torn open just under its title, with the CSS that colours the toolbox showing through at ~70% behind the tags, behind the Journey timeline a black hole sits in a wide isometric sheet of spacetime that fades away into the page (`js/spacetime.js`, loaded when the section is near); now and then something passes it on the Schwarzschild equations of motion in 3D: particles (bound orbits precess into rosettes, flybys are flung round, some fall in), light with a short wake, planets that tug on the particles and swallow those that hit them, comets with a tail streaming back along their path and, very rarely, a heavy visitor that dips the sheet, sways the hole and sends out a slowed-down quadrupole ripple, a cartoon of a gravitational wave (try `document.querySelector(".spacetime").summon("heavy")` in the console), and the BENTOS section has an animated schematic plus a sample feed (readings roll through hundredths like a digital gauge) that is labelled as a simulation, not buoy data
- Easter eggs: a glitching TM logo (focus it and press `G`), a torn-open circuit board in the footer where Snake (most often), Tetris or Pac-Man plays itself in the current theme, and an "Observer detected" readout of browser info. The readout's separate button fetches the public IP from [ipify](https://www.ipify.org/); nothing is looked up automatically or stored
- English and Polish, tuned like channels on an old TV (CH 01 EN, CH 02 PL), plus CH 03, a binary channel that writes every letter as one bit of its own character code; point at a block to decode it. Changing channel shows the channel on screen over a burst of VHS static and the text resolves out of noise. The choice is remembered, and `?lang=pl` opens the Polish page from a link. Beside them, AV puts the whole page on a worn CRT, with any channel: scanlines, an RGB phosphor mask, flicker, grain, a slow refresh band, a dark rim and a colour fringe on the text in the site's accents. On a computer, AV also brings CH 04: Spacewar!, after the 1962 PDP-1 original, round a black hole, on a round phosphor tube under the same glass (`js/spacewar.js`, loaded on first play), against the computer or a second player, with high scores kept in the browser and saved to a file on request. Like a remote, keys `1`, `2` and `3` tune the channels, `0` switches AV, `4` plays Spacewar! on AV and `Backspace` rewinds to the top
- Light / dark theme (follows the system, remembers the choice), switched with a small CRT set that folds the picture to a line and a dot (or opens it again); back-to-top is a VCR rewind key with the tape counter on screen. Responsive and accessible
- Visual inspiration: [Pokémon holographic cards by Simon Goellner](https://codepen.io/simeydotme/pen/abYWJdX) and this [terminal card](https://uiverse.io/mrhyddenn/tame-mouse-76). The CSS/JS, [faceted stars](assets/holo-stars.svg), [paper grain](assets/card-grain.svg) and [foil motifs](assets/holo/) are original; no reference code, Pokémon art or external texture assets are bundled


Run locally with `python3 -m http.server 8000`, then open `http://localhost:8000`. Serve over HTTP so the browser can load the SVG foil mask.
