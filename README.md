## 🌊 Featured: BENTOS

An open-source water-quality platform for the Gulf of Gdańsk, developed by EmbeddedSystems.do with the
[Institute of Oceanology, PAS](https://www.iopan.pl/). It brings together in-situ sensors, satellite observations and

<summary><b>🧱 About this repository</b></summary>

<br />

The source of the portfolio site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step, no dependencies.

- Hero with a live [Clifford attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_attractor) on `<canvas>` (reacts to the mouse, pauses off-screen, static with `prefers-reduced-motion`). The drifting parameters now and then brush a periodic window where particles used to merge for good and thin out to a few dots; a few are re-seeded every frame and the drift fast-forwards through a collapse
- Bento-grid projects, a BENTOS feature in the project's navy/lime branding, publications, timeline and contact
- Holographic project cards: textured cardstock, engraved foil motifs with latent images that appear under the light, and spectral reflections that follow the pointer. Touch and reduced motion keep them flat
- A collection of twenty attractor trading cards (Clifford, Peter de Jong, Simon, Ikeda, Julia, Thomas, Field–Golubitsky, Gumowski–Mira, Bedhead, McMullen, a harmonic IFS, Lorenz, Aizawa, Halvorsen, Chirikov's standard map, the logistic map's bifurcations, Hopalong, Pickover's popcorn and more), each with a classic holo foil, as on printed trading cards: in the artwork, the attractor is cut out and the space around it catches the light as holo bars, an etched fingerprint texture or sun pillars; on the frame, reverse holo and gold cards carry a texture drawn from that card's own mathematics (woven update fields, escape-time contours, flow lines…), while regular holo cards keep a matte frame. An iridescent edge ring and colour sweeping over the type finish it. The seven newest cards carry the foils of three printed rarities instead, after the techniques of Simon Goellner's [pokemon-cards-css](https://poke-holo.simey.me/): reverse holo (an etched foil drawn from the map itself all over the card but the artwork, a glint sliding with the light and a glare of its own on the matte artwork), holo rare (rainbow foil crossed by vertical beams in the artwork window) and amazing rare (the attractor breaks out of its window over the frame, in a splash of glitter). All foils are generated by `assets/deck/foil/generate.py` and, for those seven, `generate_holo.py`. The cards also show the render, the equations as iteration rules and the parameters. Cards tilt on springs and flip with a button, the mouse wheel, `F`, the arrow keys or a drag. `js/deck.js` and `css/deck.css` load only on first use; renders come from the chaosv2 notebooks, `ifs_complex.py` and `chaos_cards.py`, exported to `assets/deck/`
- A faint synthwave horizon sits behind the About text (pure CSS); now and then, at random (on average once in 200 s of watching About), night falls on it: the sun sets, peeks back up once and ducks under, pixel stars twinkle, a striped crescent moon rises, a shooting star crosses and the terminal types `sleep 8`, then the sun comes back up a little too eagerly (`js/nightfall.js`; `document.querySelector(".horizon").nightfall()` in the console); the “Currently learning” card is torn open just under its title, with the CSS that colours the toolbox showing through at ~70% behind the tags, behind the Journey timeline a black hole sits in a wide isometric sheet of spacetime that fades away into the page (`js/spacetime.js`, loaded when the section is near); now and then something passes it on the Schwarzschild equations of motion in 3D: particles (bound orbits precess into rosettes, flybys are flung round, some fall in), light with a short wake, planets that tug on the particles and swallow those that hit them, comets with a tail streaming back along their path and, at random (in the first 20 s of watching three visitors in four see one, later every few minutes), a heavy visitor that dips the sheet, sways the hole and sends out a slowed-down quadrupole ripple, a cartoon of a gravitational wave (try `document.querySelector(".spacetime").summon("heavy")` in the console). Rarer still, and just as random (about one in sixteen sees it in the first 30 s, on average one every eight minutes), comes a culmination: more and more matter gathers into a disk, the view sinks to 10° above it and closes in, and the sheet falls away while every grain of the disk is shown where its light reaches us round the hole (`js/luminet.js`: rays on Schwarzschild geodesics, direct and secondary images, the Page–Thorne flux, Doppler and gravitational shifts and the lens's magnification). Left to glow like an exposure as they orbit, the simulated particles build up Jean-Pierre Luminet's 1979 picture of a black hole with a thin accretion disk, before everything settles back (`summon("luminet")`); and the BENTOS section has an animated schematic plus a sample feed (readings roll through hundredths like a digital gauge) that is labelled as a simulation, not buoy data
- Easter eggs: a glitching TM logo (focus it and press `G`), a torn-open circuit board in the footer where Snake (most often), Tetris or Pac-Man plays itself in the current theme and now and then, at random (on average once in 90 s of play), crashes: the picture glitches, its pixels break out of the board towards the viewer and fall away, and the board stays down with a core dump on it until the page is loaded again (`js/crash.js`; `document.getElementById("footer-game").crash()` in the console), an "Observer detected" readout of browser info, and a “Currently learning” card that is not screwed down very well: knock on it three times and it comes loose screw by screw, swings on its last one and drops off the page onto a service remote, a cheat sheet for the site's rare moments. Each key winds the page there like a VCR searching a tape (FF / REW and the tape counter on screen, bands of snow rolling over the picture) and a second after PLAY sets the moment off: night over About, the Journey's heavy visitor or its culmination, a footer game of your choice or its crash, and on a computer CH 04, Spacewar! (`js/remote.js`, loaded at the first knock; not with reduced motion). The readout's separate button fetches the public IP from [ipify](https://www.ipify.org/); nothing is looked up automatically or stored
- English and Polish, tuned like channels on an old TV (CH 01 EN, CH 02 PL), plus CH 03, a binary channel that writes every letter as one bit of its own character code; point at a block to decode it. Changing channel shows the channel on screen over a burst of VHS static and the text resolves out of noise. The choice is remembered, and `?lang=pl` opens the Polish page from a link. Beside them, AV puts the whole page on a worn CRT, with any channel: scanlines, an RGB phosphor mask, flicker, grain, a slow refresh band, a dark rim and a colour fringe on the text in the site's accents. On a computer, AV also brings CH 04: Spacewar!, after the 1962 PDP-1 original, round a black hole, on a round phosphor tube (`js/spacewar.js`, loaded on first play), against the computer or a second player, with high scores kept in the browser and saved to a file on request. The console stands in front of the page, which goes out of focus behind it, and only its screens are under the AV glass; it is tuned in and out like a channel, its tube switching on from a bright line and off into a dot. The original was silent; here the sound is made in the browser from square waves and noise (`js/spacewar-sound.js`), with a volume knob on the console. Like a remote, keys `1`, `2` and `3` tune the channels, `0` switches AV, `4` plays Spacewar! on AV and `Backspace` rewinds to the top
- Light / dark theme (follows the system, remembers the choice), switched with a small CRT set that folds the picture to a line and a dot (or opens it again); back-to-top is a VCR rewind key with the tape counter on screen. Responsive and accessible
- Visual inspiration: [Pokémon holographic cards by Simon Goellner](https://codepen.io/simeydotme/pen/abYWJdX) and this [terminal card](https://uiverse.io/mrhyddenn/tame-mouse-76). The CSS/JS, [faceted stars](assets/holo-stars.svg), [paper grain](assets/card-grain.svg) and [foil motifs](assets/holo/) are original; no reference code, Pokémon art or external texture assets are bundled
